Building theThe chemistry behind East Africa's industry.

Cosotic Chemicals Industries PLC is building a 200,000 tonne-a-year integrated chlor-alkali plant in the Semera Industrial Zone, Afar Region, about 200 km from the Port of Djibouti. Using salt from Lake Afdera and ion-exchange membrane technology, it will produce caustic soda, chlorine, hydrogen, hydrochloric acid and sodium hypochlorite for Ethiopia and the region.

Cosotic Chemicals Industries PLC runs a large-scale membrane chlor-alkali plant in Ethiopia. Each year we turn salt, water and clean hydropower into 200,000 tonnes of caustic soda, plus chlorine, acids and water-treatment chemicals for manufacturers across the region.

How it works

Salt, water, power. Then five products.

An ion-exchange membrane is the core of the plant. It lets only sodium ions pass, so chlorine forms on one side and pure caustic soda and hydrogen form on the other. The process uses no mercury and no asbestos.

2NaCl+2H₂O→2NaOH+Cl₂+H₂
  1. Brine preparationSalt from Afdera is dissolved and purified to parts-per-billion hardness with ion-exchange resins.
  2. Membrane electrolysisDirect current splits the brine. Chlorine forms at the anode, and caustic soda and hydrogen form at the cathode.
  3. Caustic finishingEvaporators concentrate the lye from 32% to 50%, and a flaker turns part of it into 98% flakes.
  4. Chlorine processingChlorine is dried, compressed and liquefied, or sent to the hydrochloric acid and hypochlorite units.
  5. Hydrogen recoveryHydrogen goes into HCl synthesis and fuels the boilers. Nothing is vented as waste.

Logistics & markets

From our gate to your plant, wherever it is in the region.

Semera sits on the main highway between Addis Ababa and Djibouti, about 200 km from the Port of Djibouti. The plan replaces imports in Ethiopia and exports to Djibouti, Kenya, Sudan and South Sudan, with duty-free access under COMESA and AfCFTA.

We run our own fleet of chemical tankers. We use the Addis Ababa–Djibouti railway for containerised loads and ship through the Port of Djibouti to export markets.
